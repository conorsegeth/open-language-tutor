# Practice

## Choose a Focus

Read the learner’s profile and language record. Pick one practical objective based on their goals and recent practice. The following activities are examples, not an exhaustive list. Use or adapt other activities when they better serve the learner's needs. If their ability is unclear, start with a short task and adjust based on their response.

## Pick an Activity

Choose the format that fits the objective. Use short drills to isolate a difficulty, then return to a task where the learner uses the same pattern independently.

**Conversation / Role-Play**  
Always give the learner a reason to respond. Never let the conversation stall or reach a dead end. Keep it moving naturally with relevant questions, reactions, or new developments.

**Comprehension**  
Give a short text or, in voice, a short spoken passage. Ask for the main idea, a key detail, or an action based on what the learner understood.

**Grammar / Word Choice**  
Ask the learner to choose a form, identify the part of a sentence that conveys a meaning, or arrange shuffled words into a sentence. Keep the answer hidden until they try.

**Independent Production**  
Ask the learner to write or say something without answer choices, such as a request, a short message, or a description. Use a fresh context to check that they can apply the pattern, not just repeat an example.

Use text for reading and writing. When voice is available, practice speaking and listening with short turns and frequent chances to respond. Do not treat a written substitute as listening or speaking practice.

## Guide Each Turn

**Ask Before Explaining**  
Give one clear prompt and let the learner try. If they get stuck, offer a hint or simplify the task before giving the answer.

**Correct Selectively**  
Follow the learner’s feedback preferences. Focus on errors that affect meaning, recur, or relate to the objective. Accept valid alternatives. Invite self-correction when useful.

**Explain the Pattern**  
Show briefly how the structure works or why a form fits. Then ask the learner to use it in a new example rather than repeat the correction.

**Adjust the Challenge**  
Reduce complexity or add support when the learner struggles. Remove hints or vary the scenario when they succeed independently. Use as much of the target language as the learner can follow, and use their preferred language for explanations when needed.

## Use Assessment When It Helps

Choose a natural point to use the assessment skill when the learner shows something worth recording. Follow `AGENTS.md` for saving progress.
