# Progression Flags (Speaking & Listening)

This read-only catalog describes speaking and listening abilities by proficiency level.

## Speaking

### Beginner (A1)

- `speak_basic_greetings`: Can initiate and respond to simple greetings and leave-takings using familiar expressions.
- `speak_basic_introductions`: Can introduce themselves or another person using short, simple expressions.
- `speak_basic_personal_details`: Can give basic information about themselves in short phrases or simple sentences.
- `speak_ask_personal_questions`: Can ask simple questions to obtain basic information about another person.
- `speak_affirm_negate`: Can give clear, simple affirmative or negative answers.
- `speak_express_needs`: Can state a simple immediate need, wish, or preference.
- `speak_numbers_time`: Can express basic quantities and time information in a simple exchange.
- `speak_simple_description`: Can describe a familiar person, object, or place with a few basic words or phrases.
- `speak_repair_breakdowns`: Can request repetition, slower speech, or a simpler explanation using familiar expressions.

### Elementary (A2)

- `speak_social_exchanges`: Can carry out short, routine social exchanges using common expressions.
- `speak_describe_background`: Can give a simple description of their circumstances or background in connected short sentences.
- `speak_describe_environment`: Can describe familiar surroundings using simple language.
- `speak_describe_routines`: Can describe repeated activities and how frequently they happen.
- `speak_routine_requests`: Can make a simple request in a routine exchange.
- `speak_routine_information`: Can provide straightforward information needed to complete a routine exchange.
- `speak_routine_responses`: Can respond appropriately to a predictable request in a routine exchange.
- `speak_express_ability`: Can explain simply what they or another person can or cannot do.
- `speak_likes_dislikes`: Can state simple likes, dislikes, or preferences.
- `speak_simple_preference_reasons`: Can give a very simple reason for a preference.
- `speak_comparisons`: Can make straightforward comparisons between familiar alternatives.
- `speak_past_events`: Can give a brief account of a completed past event in simple terms.
- `speak_future_intentions`: Can describe a simple intention or plan for the future.
- `speak_simple_instructions`: Can give a short sequence of clear, straightforward directions or steps.
- `speak_clarify_basic_message`: Can restate a simple message when the listener does not understand it.

### Intermediate (B1)

- `speak_unprepared_conversation`: Can enter an unprepared conversation on a familiar subject and make relevant contributions.
- `speak_sustain_familiar_exchange`: Can maintain a familiar-topic conversation through several exchanges without constant prompting.
- `speak_narrative_connection`: Can link events into a coherent, mostly chronological spoken account.
- `speak_give_reasons`: Can explain reasons for their opinions, decisions, or plans in understandable terms.
- `speak_narrate_stories`: Can recount the main events of a story and add a personal response.
- `speak_describe_feelings`: Can describe feelings and explain what prompted them.
- `speak_basic_hypotheticals`: Can discuss a straightforward possible or hypothetical situation and its consequences.
- `speak_handle_misunderstandings`: Can ask targeted clarification questions and reformulate their own message to resolve a misunderstanding.
- `speak_compare_options`: Can compare alternatives and give a simple justification for a preference.
- `speak_manage_practical_problems`: Can explain a practical problem and negotiate a straightforward next step.
- `speak_explain_familiar_process`: Can describe the main steps in a familiar process or procedure.

### Upper Intermediate (B2)

- `speak_sustain_viewpoints`: Can state and support a position with relevant reasoning and examples.
- `speak_respond_counterarguments`: Can respond to objections and defend or qualify a position clearly.
- `speak_detailed_descriptions`: Can give a clear, detailed account that distinguishes major points from supporting details.
- `speak_explain_pros_cons`: Can evaluate alternatives by explaining their advantages, disadvantages, and trade-offs.
- `speak_abstract_discussion`: Can discuss moderately abstract issues and develop a coherent response.
- `speak_degrees_of_certainty`: Can express different degrees of certainty, obligation, and possibility clearly.
- `speak_reformulate_precisely`: Can rephrase an idea when an initial expression is unclear or imprecise.
- `speak_adjust_formality`: Can adjust their level of formality appropriately in common social or professional situations.
- `speak_structured_explanation`: Can give a well-organised extended explanation using clear links between ideas.

### Advanced (C1)

- `speak_precise_opinions`: Can express qualified opinions and distinguish subtle differences between positions.
- `speak_complex_descriptions`: Can develop a complex description with subtopics, examples, and a clear conclusion.
- `speak_integrate_contributions`: Can relate their contributions closely to others' points in a developing discussion.
- `speak_register_shifts`: Can shift register smoothly to suit purpose, audience, and relationship.
- `speak_discourse_management`: Can structure extended speech with appropriate signalling, transitions, and emphasis.
- `speak_convey_implicit_meaning`: Can deliberately convey implication or subtle emphasis without stating everything directly.
- `speak_idiomatic_language`: Can use a range of idiomatic or colloquial expressions naturally and appropriately.
- `speak_handle_complex_disagreement`: Can handle nuanced disagreement or sensitive points diplomatically while maintaining clarity.

### Proficient (C2)

- `speak_stylistic_command`: Can organise complex spoken material for maximum clarity, coherence, and rhetorical effect.
- `speak_cultural_competence`: Can adapt spoken language sensitively to subtle social, pragmatic, and cultural expectations.
- `speak_ambiguity_nuance`: Can use ambiguity, indirectness, or figurative language deliberately and effectively.
- `speak_complex_argumentation`: Can integrate competing perspectives, counterarguments, and qualifications into a persuasive spoken case.
- `speak_adapt_style_flexibly`: Can shift smoothly between nuanced formal, neutral, and informal speaking styles.
- `speak_control_humour`: Can use humour, irony, or allusion naturally without obscuring the intended meaning.

## Listening

### Beginner (A1)

- `listen_isolated_words`: Can recognise familiar words in slow, clearly articulated speech.
- `listen_basic_phrases`: Can understand short, familiar expressions in slow, clearly articulated speech.
- `listen_personal_questions`: Can understand simple questions addressed directly to them about familiar information when spoken slowly.
- `listen_simple_statements`: Can understand short, direct statements about concrete situations when spoken slowly.
- `listen_affirmation_negation`: Can distinguish straightforward affirmative and negative spoken statements.
- `listen_basic_numbers_times`: Can recognise basic quantities and time expressions in clear speech.
- `listen_single_step_instruction`: Can follow a single short spoken direction or instruction when delivered slowly and clearly.
- `listen_repeated_information`: Can identify the meaning of a simple utterance when it is repeated or rephrased clearly.

### Elementary (A2)

- `listen_high_frequency_phrases`: Can understand common phrases about familiar situations in clearly articulated speech.
- `listen_simple_messages_main_point`: Can identify the main purpose of a short, clear spoken message.
- `listen_simple_messages_details`: Can extract explicitly stated key details from a short, clear spoken message.
- `listen_routine_exchanges`: Can follow a predictable back-and-forth exchange when participants speak clearly.
- `listen_simple_directions`: Can follow a short sequence of clearly delivered spoken instructions.
- `listen_simple_descriptions`: Can understand straightforward spoken descriptions of familiar people, objects, or situations.
- `listen_simple_event_sequence`: Can follow the basic order of events in a short, simply told account.
- `listen_simple_preferences`: Can recognise explicitly expressed likes, dislikes, or preferences in clear speech.
- `listen_simple_requests`: Can understand routine requests, offers, and responses spoken clearly and directly.

### Intermediate (B1)

- `listen_standard_familiar_speech`: Can identify the main points of clear, standard speech on familiar or personally relevant subjects.
- `listen_familiar_speech_details`: Can understand important supporting details in clear, structured speech on familiar subjects.
- `listen_extended_familiar_discussion`: Can follow the main thread of a reasonably structured discussion on familiar subjects.
- `listen_speaker_views`: Can distinguish explicitly stated opinions from explanations or factual information in clear speech.
- `listen_narrative_sequence`: Can follow the chronology and main causal links in a straightforward spoken account.
- `listen_explanations_reasons`: Can follow a simple spoken explanation of reasons, consequences, or choices.
- `listen_technical_instructions`: Can understand spoken procedural instructions for a familiar type of task when clearly presented.
- `listen_common_reformulations`: Can keep up when a speaker corrects, repeats, or rephrases a point in everyday conversation.

### Upper Intermediate (B2)

- `listen_extended_argument`: Can follow a sustained line of reasoning in clearly delivered extended speech.
- `listen_contrasting_viewpoints`: Can distinguish and follow contrasting positions within a spoken discussion.
- `listen_detailed_explanations`: Can understand detailed explanations and qualifications on a broad range of subjects.
- `listen_complex_instructions_familiar`: Can follow multi-step spoken instructions with conditions and qualifications in a familiar context.
- `listen_discourse_connections`: Can recognise cause, contrast, concession, and conclusions across extended speech.

### Advanced (C1)

- `listen_unstructured_speech`: Can follow extended speech whose organization is loose or not explicitly signposted.
- `listen_implicit_relationships`: Can infer connections between ideas that are suggested rather than stated.
- `listen_idiomatic_colloquial`: Can understand a broad range of idiomatic and colloquial expressions in context.
- `listen_register_shifts`: Can recognise shifts in formality, stance, or social register during speech.
- `listen_subtext_irony`: Can recognise implicit attitudes, subtext, or irony when supported by context.
- `listen_dense_explanations`: Can follow dense, complex explanations, including some unfamiliar concepts.
- `listen_abstract_discussion`: Can follow extended discussion of abstract or conceptually demanding issues.
- `listen_specialised_discussion_familiar`: Can follow a complex discussion within a field they know reasonably well.

### Proficient (C2)

- `listen_specialised_discourse`: Can follow specialised discourse even when its subject is unfamiliar, drawing on contextual cues.
- `listen_ambiguity_allusion`: Can recognise ambiguity, allusion, and indirect references in complex speech.
- `listen_humour_irony`: Can interpret sophisticated humour, irony, and rhetorical effects in spoken context.
- `listen_styles_registers`: Can understand nuanced meaning across contrasting speaking styles and levels of formality.
